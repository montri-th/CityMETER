Allocated and disbursed: reading the 2015 budget through places

Government Budget & Disbursement

For geographically matched fiscal-year 2015 records, CityMETER displays an adjusted budget of 59,344.5 million baht and disbursements of 40,831.4 million baht, or 68.8%. Reading them together opens a practical question: which stage of the spending process does each amount describe?

Two stages in the same financial story

An adjusted budget and a recorded disbursement describe different stages. Keep the column name beside the amount when reading a place-based comparison. The PBO manual describes GFMIS records with separate fields for adjusted budgets, disbursements and purchase-order commitments. Those distinctions give a project enquiry a clearer starting point. A reader can identify which figure prompted the question and then look for information about that stage of the work. The percentage becomes easier to discuss because its two underlying amounts remain visible.

A place gives the budget enquiry an address

A place-based view helps turn a broad spending question into a manageable enquiry. Begin with the year and area connected to the work, then examine the agencies or project types attached to the available items. For someone preparing a conversation about community services, the useful result may be a short list of records to read in more detail. Each item can lead to a more focused request for information. The map supplies a way into the material, while the financial labels keep the discussion tied to the right stage.

Keep the year and unit attached to the comparison

The displayed amounts are in million baht. Carrying that unit and the fiscal year into a note or presentation preserves the meaning of the figures. Before comparing places, decide whether the enquiry concerns adjusted amounts, disbursements or the relationship between the two. Apply that choice consistently and keep the relevant item names alongside the comparison. This makes the work easier for a colleague to follow. They can return to the same records and understand which part of the financial picture led to the next question.

Follow the money with a specific question about the work

After reading the figures, select one item connected to the issue being studied and continue into its project documents or agency reports. Ask what the work involves, which period it covers and what information describes its progress. These questions connect a financial record to the activity that people want to understand. The 2015 example offers a concrete starting point for that sequence. It helps a reader move from a large amount on a screen to an identifiable record and a question that the responsible organisation can address.
